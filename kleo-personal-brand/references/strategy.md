# Positioning, content strategy and idea development

The methods below are this skill's operating design. They are informed by a knowledge/voice/strategy workflow, not presented as Kleo's hidden algorithm.

## Build a point of view tied to the offer

Turn discovery into a short strategic brief:

1. **Audience:** a specific person in a specific situation, not “business owners” without context.
2. **Problem:** a recurring problem that the author can explain credibly.
3. **Useful outcome:** what progress the audience wants, with no invented guarantees.
4. **Distinct approach:** a mechanism, tradeoff or belief the author can demonstrate.
5. **Proof:** what evidence currently exists and what is still missing.
6. **Positioning:** the association the author wants readers to remember.
7. **Next step:** one real conversion or relationship path aligned with the goal.

Working sentence: “For [audience in situation], [author] helps with [problem/outcome] through [approach], grounded in [evidence].” Do not publish bracketed placeholders. If the brief is uncertain, offer two reasoned positioning options and proceed with a labeled assumption for drafts. Only the client can establish unsupported personal beliefs or promises.

Product features become content through customer decisions: feature → useful mechanism → scenario → tradeoff/proof → lesson. For example, an approval queue can support a post about where human review still matters in automation. It does not prove that a customer reduced costs or headcount.

## Content pillars and purposes

Choose normally 3–5 pillars that the author has enough evidence to sustain. Each pillar needs an audience problem, an author-specific angle, available proof, relation to the offer and topics to avoid. Adjust the count to the actual niche and request.

Separate **topic** from **purpose**:

| Purpose | Useful content | Evidence needed | Meaningful response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Clear observation, timely insight, recognizable problem | Accurate context + relevant viewpoint | Right people notice/follow |
| Trust | Tutorial, decision framework, tradeoff, original method | Specific reasoning, examples or demonstration | Relevant questions, saves if available, repeat attention |
| Proof | Case study, teardown, outcome explanation | Permission, baseline, scope, dates and result | Qualified buyer confidence |
| Connection | Real story, working notes, lessons learned | Authentic client account + public-use permission | Recognition and substantive conversation |
| Action | Offer fit, objection handling, useful resource, invitation | Accurate offer and an actual next step | Qualified inquiry, opt-in or product action |

Do not enforce a universal content ratio. Choose a starting mix from goal, maturity, evidence and capacity; label it a hypothesis. A new author with little proof can teach and demonstrate instead of inventing case studies. A small specialist audience may be more valuable than broad viral reach.

## Generate ideas from evidence

Mine the knowledge base for decisions, mistakes, recurring objections, surprising comparisons, process steps, before/after changes, questions from customers and views the author can defend. Select a single tension or useful insight per post.

An idea card contains: audience problem, working angle, author-specific contribution, evidence/source IDs, purpose, pillar, proposed format, takeaway, optional next step and status. A subject such as “AI for sales” is a topic, not a complete idea.

Rank ideas using a small editorial rubric, 0–2 each:

- Audience relevance: generic / plausible / clearly linked to a known problem.
- Credibility: no basis / supported reasoning / first-hand or verified evidence.
- Distinctiveness: interchangeable / specific angle / recognizable author insight.
- Goal fit: unclear / supporting / direct relation to current objective.
- Readiness: missing essentials / minor gaps / usable now.

Prefer ready ideas scoring at least 7/10, but do not let a score rescue an unsupported claim. Keep promising evidence-poor ideas in a research/interview queue. Diversify across pillars and purposes; five hooks around the same insight are one idea with variants.

## Four-week planning default

When the user asks for an ongoing strategy without a schedule, use a four-week planning horizon and explicitly mark the cadence as provisional. A manageable starting assumption is three posts weekly; adapt to the author's actual capacity, audience and feedback. Daily content is an option only when requested and sustainable.

For every slot specify: date or relative week/day, pillar, purpose, distinct angle, format, evidence needed, CTA if appropriate, owner, status and measurement hypothesis. If exact scheduling is requested, resolve the client's timezone before finalizing dates. Do not invent a best posting time or present a default as algorithmic advice.

Batch related work: collect evidence → choose angles → draft → edit → prepare assets → review → schedule if authorized. Maintain room for a relevant current event or a new client insight. Do not replace the whole plan every time a trend appears.

Avoid consecutive posts with the same hook shape, CTA, narrative arc or conclusion. Map the sequence so a reader learns something new while the positioning remains recognizable.

When choosing structures for a batch, use [template-library.md](template-library.md) after the ideas and evidence are clear. Map each slot's purpose to a retrieval goal, preserve the actual client pillar, and check recent template/opening IDs. Do not create a calendar by assigning templates first and inventing stories to fill them. The index is not a required content mix or a performance ranking.

## Personal-brand development beyond posts

Align the profile promise, content themes, proof and destination. A reader who clicks through should find the same expertise and a coherent next step. If the product/offer is unclear, write a specific clarification question and use supported educational angles meanwhile.

Human connection can include thoughtful participation in relevant conversations. Prepare comments only in response to the real post, adding a useful idea or question; do not manufacture familiarity or mass-generate interchangeable praise. Sending comments or DMs requires explicit authorization under the host's rules. No automated engagement, bulk outreach or contact harvesting is part of the default workflow.

For a requested lead magnet, define one audience job and create a useful resource/outline connected to the offer. An email capture page, automated delivery or follow-up needs available tools and separate task authorization. Do not claim that a document alone is a working lead funnel.

## Deliver the strategy as a usable plan

Provide the positioning, audience/offer fit, pillars with examples, idea queue, schedule, starter drafts requested or implied by full setup, and the first experiment. Explain the two or three choices that matter. Save this as the current strategy with date, source references and assumptions. Do not bury the user in a generic branding lecture.

Review at the agreed interval using actual evidence. Record changes as hypotheses or supported decisions, including what stayed the same and why. A low-performing post alone does not establish that the topic, product or voice is wrong.
